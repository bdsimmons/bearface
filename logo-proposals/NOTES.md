# Bearface logo proposals — round 2

All three marks are single-color, text-free SVGs on a `0 0 100 100` viewBox. Every visible shape inherits `currentColor`, so each proposal works unchanged in ink, cobalt, or white.

## Candidate A — Refined profile badge

The priority proposal preserves the original mark's essential idea: an angular six-sided field with the bear removed from it. The redraw tightens the outside contour, simplifies the profile, and exaggerates the important small-size cues—the ear break, brow-to-muzzle line, and open neck. It is recognizably descended from the 2016 identity, but cleaner and more architectural.

## Candidate B — Divided profile

Two poised planes stop just short of depicting the animal directly. Their facing edges describe the crown, muzzle, jaw, and neck; the eye completes the bear in the negative interval. This keeps the original's figure/ground intelligence without a containing badge and gives the brand a broader, more horizontal stance.

## Candidate C — Signal profile

The wildcard reduces the bear to three directional facets. The central stroke carries the muzzle, jaw, and shoulder; the separated crown and baseline imply ear and mass without enclosing a face. It reads first as a precise signal or forward-moving device, then resolves into a side-profile bear—an intentionally restrained fit for the site's technical voice.

## Recommended direction

Candidate A is the strongest continuity choice and should advance first. It owns the most distinctive equity from the original while correcting its softness and reducing the profile to features that survive at 24px. Candidate B is the best alternate if the badge itself feels too tied to the earlier identity; Candidate C is the most radical system mark.
